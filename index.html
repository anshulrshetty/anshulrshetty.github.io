01 / MSc dissertation

Formula One vehicle dynamics & setup modelling

Oxford Brookes University · 2026

Simulation · Vehicle dynamics · Data analysis

For my dissertation, I wanted to understand how much confidence to place in lap-time simulation when deciding between vehicle setups. I developed a 2026-focused Formula One model in AVL VSM and studied suspension and vehicle response at Silverstone and Spa. I also built a Python pipeline around FastF1 to analyse driver behaviour from public telemetry.

I ran 17 one-factor-at-a-time studies at Silverstone and a 36-candidate multivariable study at Spa. Then I held back 16 simulation runs per circuit to test the models on combinations they had never seen. On those blind simulation tests, the lap-time prediction error was 0.0210 s RMSE at Silverstone and 0.0186 s at Spa.

Real F1 timing was harder to match. Rather than present the model as an accurate predictor of actual F1 lap times, I used it to compare setup responses. Learning where a model stops being reliable was as useful as learning where it works.

AVL VSM  ·  FastF1  ·  Python  ·  Design of experiments  ·  Model testing
02 / Personal software project

PITWALL: making more sense of my telemetry

PITWALL began with my own Assetto Corsa Competizione sessions. I wanted to stop comparing only lap times and start looking at what changed through braking, corner entry, steering and throttle application.

I built a Python desktop app to read MoTeC telemetry, handle channels recorded at different rates and organise lap data by corner. The initial Silverstone campaign used an Aston Martin V8 Vantage GT3 and produced 217 corner observations from one driver. More drivers and cars are needed before I can make stronger claims about its recommendations.

Python  ·  PySide6  ·  MoTeC  ·  pandas  ·  GitHub Actions
03 / Formula Student

Finding the right tyre for OBR

Oxford Brookes Racing needed to compare 6-inch and 7.5-inch tyres for its Formula Student car. I worked through Formula SAE Tire Test Consortium data in MATLAB and fitted Pacejka Magic Formula models to understand the differences.

The 7.5-inch option showed more lateral-force potential, with pressure and camber sensitivity to consider. I recommended it, and the team adopted that choice for OBR26. I also helped make the team’s simulation procedures easier to follow and repeat.

MATLAB  ·  TTC data  ·  Pacejka tyre modelling  ·  Vehicle dynamics

Other projects and engineering work

04IDSS: setup and race-strategy decision support+

I built IDSS so I could navigate my dissertation findings without searching through individual plots and spreadsheets. The interface brings together circuit and driver selection, event-specific driver characteristics, setup recommendations and the reasons behind each choice.

I tested whether public telemetry could identify stable driver categories, but those categories did not pass my consistency checks. The final approach describes the driver in the current event instead. I also added a separate race-strategy module. Its selected stop count matched the most common observed dry-race stop count in 13 of the 14 completed 2026 events studied. Driver-specific tyre degradation was left out after a held-out test did not support it.

Python · FastF1 · Streamlit · Setup selection · Strategy modelling

05Circuit geometry builder+

Getting realistic circuit geometry into AVL VSM was a project in itself. I built a Python tool to process track centrelines and elevation data, assess curvature and generate simulation-ready road geometry.

For Spa I used a TUMFTM centreline with banking derived from SPW LiDAR data. I also reconstructed Monaco's centreline and elevation, but excluded it from setup-response simulation because I couldn't establish a reliable banking and crossfall source.

Python · SciPy · Circuit reconstruction · Geometry checks

06Race strategy and fuel-sensitive stint modelling+

I developed a 52-lap Silverstone strategy model to explore tyre compounds, degradation, fuel mass and pit timing. A reference one-stop Hard-to-Soft scenario used a 20.5 s pit-loss assumption, 70 kg starting fuel and a 0.035 s/kg fuel sensitivity.

The strategy work later became part of the dissertation, where I tested stop-count outputs against completed 2026 races rather than treating an assumed strategy as a real-world result.

Python · Stint modelling · Fuel effects · Tyre degradation

07Smart tyre pressure control system+

I led a team of four to build a tyre pressure control prototype using TPMS sensors, an ECU and pneumatic valves. We used feedback to adjust pressure automatically instead of relying on manual correction.

In prototype testing, the system achieved control within ±0.3 bar. The work placed second at Technova 2023–24, received KSCST funding and led to an Indian provisional patent application, IN20254105002.

Embedded control · Pneumatics · Sensor integration · SolidWorks

08SAEINDIA mBAJA: suspension and steering+

At Team SJEC Racing, I contributed to double-wishbone suspension and steering design for the SAEINDIA mBAJA vehicle. My work covered CAD, suspension geometry, steering calculations and technical documentation. I also supported the IPG CarMaker virtual-round work and used Lotus Suspension during the geometry study.

SolidWorks · Lotus Suspension · IPG CarMaker · CAD

09Autonomous wheelchair and guide bot+

During my undergraduate studies, I worked on an autonomous wheelchair and an autonomous guide bot with multidisciplinary student teams, concentrating on mechanical design and prototype development. The wheelchair project won first place at the 2023 NITTE Healthcare Innovation Hackathon.
